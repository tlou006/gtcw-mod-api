# Commanders

## 1. What the five commander stats do

Every commander has five stats, shown on a 0–100 scale: **Leadership**, **Initiative**, **Administration**, **Cunning** and **Fame**. Here is what each one actually does.

> **The stat number on the portrait is a ceiling, not what you get.** A commander with no experience works at only **half** his listed stats, and reaches the full value only at full experience. A wounded or captured commander is halved again. So an 80-leadership general with little experience can perform worse than a 60-leadership veteran.

&nbsp;

### Leadership: keeps your men in the fight

**Best for:** brigade commanders, and any corps you expect to be pushed back.

* **Raises fighting spirit**, the hard cap on how high a unit's morale can go in battle. It is measured against your own army's average, so a leader only helps if he is better than his peers. Leadership matters most for brigade commanders: there it counts **4×** as much as fame, while for army commanders it counts only a quarter as much.
* **Shrinks every morale loss.** Morale losses in battle are multiplied by **3 to 5**, depending on how experienced the unit is and how good its commander's leadership is. A 100-leadership general takes a full point off that multiplier, for example from ×4 down to ×3, which means **a quarter less morale lost**.
* **Lifts the morale of nearby units.** Having a commander close by boosts morale, and leadership counts for about **1.7×** as much as fame here. If he is killed, leadership counts **2.5×** as much as fame in the morale shock that follows.
* **Lets units take heavier casualties** before their losses count as "unbearable".
* **Softens a unit's first battle.** A unit that has never fought is capped at 60% morale; a top leader buys back up to **20 points** of that.
* **Cuts losses on a retreat.** A retreating unit loses extra men. Under a 90-leadership general it loses about **6% extra**; under a 30-leadership one, about **42% extra**. No other stat does this.
* **Naval firepower.** An admiral's leadership raises his fleet's firepower against both ships and forts.

### Initiative: reach, tempo and aggression

**Best for:** your commander-in-chief, and formations that need to move and cover ground.

* **Widens command range and combat range** of every formation on the campaign map. Command range decides how much of your army is within a commander's influence at all, so this matters a lot.
* **Slows the readiness drain from marching.** At 100 initiative, readiness wears down at only about **a third** of the normal rate. But no formation can do better here than your **commander-in-chief's** initiative allows, so a cautious C-in-C holds back your whole army.
* **Makes AI generals bolder.** A cautious AI general needs about **80%** of your strength before it attacks. A 100-initiative one will attack at **less than half** (47%), and will launch an all-out assault at **1.76 to 1** odds where a cautious one waits for **3 to 1**. Bold AI generals also launch offensives on the campaign map more readily.
* **At sea:** fleets start engagements from further away and try to ram more often.
* **Causes feuds.** A subordinate whose initiative is very different from his superior's, **higher or lower**, will start feuding. A 40-point gap gives about a **10% chance per hour**, so a feud within a day is all but certain. While feuding, the formation is taken out of your hands, its orders take **3×** as long, and it may charge the nearest enemy on its own. **Political** generals are twice as likely to charge off as West Pointers, and four times as likely as volunteers.

### Administration: logistics and fleets

**Best for:** large armies, and admirals.

* **Faster readiness recovery**, up to **a third faster** at 100.
* **Better supply lines during a battle.** This does not affect supply on the campaign map. As an example, a brigade at the bottom of the chain might get **72%** supply under a 50-administration commander but only **62%** under a 0-administration one.
* **Fewer overnight desertions.** Administration and fame count equally here.
* **Less fatigue** when men and horses are in poor condition.
* **Fleets move, repair and resupply faster.**
* **Larger fleets.** An admiral at 100 administration can command **twice** as many ships as one at 0. Unusually, experience does not count here: a green admiral can command as many ships as a veteran with the same rating.

### Cunning: scouting, raiding and cavalry

**Best for:** cavalry, and any detached command.

* **Intelligence gathering.** Cunning has the strongest effect here of any stat on anything in the game.
* **Concealment.** Your formation is harder for the enemy to spot.
* **Raiding and guarding** are more effective.
* **Sieges** are more effective.
* **Fewer losses when skirmishing**, up to **25% fewer** at 100 (in auto-resolved battles).
* **Cavalry take fewer casualties** under a cunning commander (in auto-resolved battles). It is the only stat that directly saves men in combat.
* **Garrisons slip away** instead of being captured.
* **Cunning AI generals burn their depots** rather than let you capture them.

&nbsp;
### Fame: reputation as a stat

**Best for:** army commanders, and political generals.

* **Raises fighting spirit**, just like leadership, but fame matters most at the **top** of the chain: for army commanders it counts **4×** as much as leadership.
* **Fewer overnight desertions**, equal to administration.
* **A small morale boost to nearby units**, worth less than half of what leadership gives.
* **Generates state support, but only for political generals.** A political general produces state support while he holds a command, and fame multiplies it by up to **3.5×**.
* **Decides promotions and succession.** Fame feeds the ranking that decides who gets promoted, who replaces a fallen commander, and who becomes commander-in-chief.
* **Causes feuds.** A subordinate who is **more famous** than his superior is more likely to feud. A less famous one isn't.
* **Famous and defamed status.** After each battle, the best performer can become **famous** (fame counts **×1.4**) and the worst can become **defamed** (fame counts **×0.7**). Defame wears off after about five months.

### Which stats can improve?

| Stat | Can it grow? |
| --- | --- |
| **Experience** | Yes, about 20 points per year of service, up to your nation's military experience level |
| **Fame** | Yes, through battles, promotions and events. Drops 5–30% each time he comes back from a wound |
| **Initiative** | Only down: a commander returning from a wound loses 5–30% of it |
| **Leadership** | No |
| **Administration** | No |
| **Cunning** | No |

> **The one exception: military schools.** Building military schools in a commander's **home state** slowly raises his leadership, administration, cunning and initiative all at once. Nothing he does in the field will ever improve them. These gains also don't show up in the stat breakdown tooltip.
